Classifying satellite images of land use

Two neural networks trained from scratch on EuroSAT, 64×64 tiles from the Sentinel-2 satellite. Each tile shows one of ten kinds of land. One network is a multilayer perceptron (MLP), the other a convolutional network (CNN).

Try it

Pick a tile and both models guess its class. Every number below the image is the probability the model gives to that class.

or drop an image here

The image is cropped to a square from its centre and scaled down to 64×64. The models only saw Sentinel-2 satellite tiles in training. An ordinary photo still gets one of the 10 classes, but that answer means nothing.

Data

EuroSAT RGB (Helber et al., 2019), from Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.7711810. Each class is split on its own with a fixed seed: 80% for training and 20% for test. The last tenth of the training part is a validation set used only for the training curves.

The first test tile of every class

Under each tile is what the two models say. Click a tile to try it.

Models

A model is a chain of steps. A tile goes in at the top, and ten scores come out at the bottom, one per class. The highest score is the model's guess. The little picture next to each step shows the shape of the data at that point: a square is still an image, a stack of squares is many images on top of each other, and a bar is a plain list of numbers.

Where the parameters go

A parameter is one number the model adjusts while it learns. Both bars use the same scale, and each segment is one step from the diagrams above.
